Physical vs. Logical Diagrams

Guiding question: How do physical and logical network diagrams help in designing, managing, and troubleshooting networks? A physical diagram shows the actual hardware and where the cables go. A logical diagram shows how data flows and how systems interact, regardless of where anything sits.

Physical Diagrams

HARDWARE · CABLES · SPACE

Show the actual hardware setup and how devices are physically connected. Used for installation, maintenance, and troubleshooting.

"Where is it, and what is it plugged into?"

Examples · click to explore

Use cases

  • Installation & maintenance: helps technicians locate devices and cables.
  • Troubleshooting: identifies physical connection issues.
  • Planning: organize space, optimize airflow, design the layout.

Logical Diagrams

DATA FLOW · ADDRESSING

Focus on how data moves between network devices and how systems interact, independent of physical layout.

"How does traffic get from A to B?"

Rack Diagram Cheat Sheet

Purpose

  • Visualizes how servers, switches, and routers are arranged in racks
  • Manages space so devices are organized efficiently
  • Gives technicians a clear layout for servicing hardware

Components

  • Rack units (U): each device's height
  • Cable management: how cables are routed in the rack
  • Power & cooling: power strips, UPS units, fans

Types

  • Front view: devices as seen from the front
  • Side view: depth and stacking of devices
  • Detailed: models and cable connections

Layer 2 Diagram Cheat Sheet

Purpose

Shows how devices within the same network segment connect and how data transfers between them. Details switches and VLANs.

Components

  • Switches and the MAC addresses they learn
  • VLANs that segment traffic
  • Logical connections via switches and VLANs

Use cases

  • Design: plan VLANs
  • Troubleshoot: problems inside the same LAN
  • Configure: set up switches and VLANs
